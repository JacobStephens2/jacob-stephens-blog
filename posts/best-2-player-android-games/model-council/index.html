Comprehensive Analysis

High-confidence findings. All three models independently converged on the same three "same room, two devices" pillars: Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes, Spaceteam, and BombSquad. That convergence is meaningful because each model reached it from a different angle — GPT-5.6 Sol Thinking ranked by design intent, Claude Opus 5 Thinking ranked by fit to a stated aversion to shared screens, and Kimi K3 ranked by setup friction. Keep Talking is the strongest of the three for a duo specifically: it is marketed as in-person-only with no online mode at all, and you only need one purchased copy since the defusal manual can live on a second phone, a laptop, or a printout. It also maps almost exactly onto the asymmetric-information tension already enjoyed in The Resistance and Diplomacy.[2][27][1]

Spaceteam and BombSquad both scale past two, which is their strength and their weakness. Spaceteam gives each player their own control panel over local Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, satisfying the "everyone on their own device" rule, and it's free. Claude Opus 5 Thinking added the important caveat the others missed: the Play Store build has stalled at 3.0.2 with parts of the game broken and the developer distributing unlock codes manually. Install and test it before you build an evening around it. BombSquad is the physics-chaos option — genuinely great, but two players is below its sweet spot.[3][4][6][17][18][7]

Areas of divergence. The sharpest split is philosophical: Kimi K3 interpreted "same room" as "share my phone," crowning Duel Otters, a game built for two thumbs on opposite ends of one screen. GPT-5.6 Sol Thinking and Claude Opus 5 Thinking both read the situation as two people with two phones and landed on The Past Within, a Rusty Lake point-and-click where one player is in the past and one in the future, neither can see the other's screen, and progress comes entirely from describing what you see out loud. Given a documented preference for non-shared-screen multiplayer, the two-device reading is almost certainly correct — but Kimi's answer isn't wrong, it's the right answer for the one-phone-at-a-bar case, which does come up.[22][9][28][24]

The second divergence is about replay depth. GPT-5.6 Sol Thinking pushed Polytopia and Star Realms as the long-tail competitive picks, while Claude Opus 5 Thinking argued 7 Wonders Duel and Jaipur are the better fit because they're tighter two-player-native designs with official pass'n'play. Claude has the better argument for this taste profile — 7 Wonders Duel and Duel for Middle-Earth were already flagged as interests, and Jaipur is a ten-minute duel with the scoring automated. Star Realms is the compromise: free to start, two-player-only by design, pass-and-play supported, and closest to poker's tempo-and-risk feel.[13][14][16][29]

There's also a real distinction the models surfaced only partially: which of these actually work with zero connectivity. Tick Tock: A Tale for Two is the cleanest here — both players just pick "Player 1" or "Player 2" and the game never talks to the other device at all, since the entire sync happens through your voices. That makes it the single best airplane pick, and it's the closest shipped analogue to the architecture being built for Clave. The tradeoff, which Claude Opus 5 Thinking flagged and the others didn't emphasize enough, is that both Tick Tock and The Past Within require two purchased copies with no split-screen fallback.[30][10][31]

Unique insights worth noting. Claude Opus 5 Thinking was the only model to connect this query back to the Clave project by surfacing Bounden — two players hold opposite ends of one phone and rotate it through choreography designed by the Dutch National Ballet. It is essentially the only shipped mobile game that encodes partner dance as a two-player loop, so it's worth playing as design research regardless of whether you enjoy it. Its Play Store availability has been inconsistent, so verify the listing first. Claude also caught Chess.com's "Play in Person" hotseat mode, which is the lowest-friction possible option given a fiancée who plays chess.[21][19][20]

Recommendation. Install three things: Tick Tock: A Tale for Two for a full offline evening on two phones (and as direct prior art for Clave), 7 Wonders Duel or Jaipur as the permanent pass-and-play filler on one phone, and Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes for when you want the loud, adrenal version. Follow The Past Within after Tick Tock if the asymmetric-co-op format lands — it's the deeper of the two. Grab Duel Otters as the free zero-setup fallback for one-phone situations, and test Spaceteam before committing to it socially.
